In·tro·it, n. From the Latin introitus, an entrance. The psalm sung as the Divine Service begins.

Where a Visitor Begins

The Introit is where the Divine Service begins. For most visitors now, a parish's website is where they begin: the service times, which door, whether they may commune. Introit gives a congregation a website that answers those questions and looks like it belongs to a church.

What a Parish Gets

One service with everything in it: the hosting, every page, the console, and the parish's own domain.

The Church Year, kept for you

The season and its colour, the name of the day, and the readings appointed in Lutheran Service Book, in the one-year or the three-year series. Nobody types any of it.

The pages a parish needs

Home, Our Parish, Worship, Visiting, Sermons, Calendar and Ministries, in the words a Lutheran congregation actually uses, with room for pages of its own.

The questions a visitor asks

Times of service, where to park and which door, and the communion practice in the parish's own words.

A console anyone can use

Plain forms, no code, and nothing that can break the page. A change is live the moment it is saved.

The calendar the office already keeps

Read from Google or Outlook every morning, so nothing is typed twice.

The parish's own address

Its own domain name if it has one, and an Introit address that never stops working if it has not.

What It Will Not Do

  • No tracking, no advertising, and nothing sold about the people who visit.
  • No pop-ups, no chat bubble, and no box asking for an email address.
  • No script on the page, so it loads quickly on an old phone in the parking lot.
  • No stock photographs. A parish's own pictures, or none.
  • No holding on. The words and the pictures are the parish's, and so is its domain.
